## Description

ADR-0005 says all money is `decimal` and rounds half away from zero via `Money.Round`
(`MidpointRounding.AwayFromZero`). Two places do not follow it:

1. **Pricing:** `QuoteCalculator.ApplyFuelSurcharge` calls `Math.Round(value, 2)`, whose
   default is banker's rounding (`MidpointRounding.ToEven`). Everything else in pricing
   uses `Money.Round`.
2. **Web:** `roundMoney(amount)` in `kestrel-web/src/lib/money.ts` is
   `Math.round(amount * 100) / 100`. That works on binary floats, so `1.005 * 100` is
   `100.49999999999999` and rounds down, and `Math.round` rounds halves toward positive
   infinity, so negative halves differ from AwayFromZero.

Finance found it when a 7.5% surcharge on 135.00 (exactly 10.125) priced at 10.12 on the
invoice line but 10.13 in the carrier statement.

## Acceptance criteria

1. `ApplyFuelSurcharge` rounds through `Money.Round`.
2. xunit regression: base 135.00 at `Pricing:FuelSurchargePct` 7.5 yields 10.13; also
   0.005 yields 0.01.
3. Web rounding uses integer minor units or a decimal library with half-away-from-zero
   semantics; `roundMoney(1.005)` equals 1.01 and `roundMoney(-2.505)` equals -2.51.
4. vitest cases for both.
5. A note added to ADR-0005 closing the "known deviations" paragraph.
6. `dotnet test` and `npm test` are green.

## Impact of changing quotes

`/v1/quote` amounts may change by one minor unit in rare half-way cases. Consumers are the
gateway (`/api/quote`, platform-team) and web (web-team); both are informed.
